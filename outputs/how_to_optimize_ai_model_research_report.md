**Title**  
The Influence of Perceived Predictive Authority on Human Decision‑Making: Implications for Human‑Centred AI Model Design  

---

**Abstract**  
Recent behavioural research shows that when artificial‑intelligence (AI) systems are perceived as capable of predicting an individual’s own actions, they can reshape the reasoning process, narrowing the set of futures that decision‑makers consider and reducing realised earnings. In a Newcomb‑type experiment with 1,305 participants, more than 40 % treated the AI predictor as authoritative, leading to a 3.39‑fold increase in the odds of rejecting a guaranteed reward (95 % CI = 2.45–4.70) and a 10.7 %–42.9 % reduction in earnings relative to a baseline without AI predictions. The effect persisted across presentation formats, decision contexts, and even when the AI’s predictions repeatedly failed. This paper reviews these findings, details the experimental methodology, evaluates alternative explanations, and discusses how the results inform human‑centred criteria for optimizing AI models that interact with decision‑makers.  

---

### 1. Introduction  
AI systems increasingly act as predictive partners in recommendation engines, autonomous agents, and large‑language models. While the primary aim of such systems is to improve decision quality, the mere presence of a prediction can alter the decision environment itself. Understanding how *perceived predictive authority*—the extent to which users regard an AI’s forecast as reliable—affects human reasoning is essential for designing AI that supports, rather than unintentionally constrains, human agency.
